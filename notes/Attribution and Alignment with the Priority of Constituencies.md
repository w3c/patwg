# Attribution and Alignment with the Priority of Constituencies

\> The internet is for end users: any change made to the web platform has the potential to affect vast numbers of people, and may have a profound impact on any person’s life. \[RFC8890\]  
\>   
\> User needs come before the needs of web page authors, which come before the needs of user agent implementors, which come before the needs of specification writers, which come before theoretical purity.  
\>   
\> Like all principles, this isn’t absolute. Ease of authoring affects how content reaches users. User agents have to prioritize finite engineering resources, which affects how features reach authors. Specification writers also have finite resources, and theoretical concerns reflect underlying needs of all of these groups.

\- [Web Platform Design Principles](https://w3ctag.github.io/design-principles/) \- [1.1. Put user needs first (Priority of Constituencies)](https://w3ctag.github.io/design-principles/#priority-of-constituencies)

The web is fundamentally a utopian project. It always has been. The maintenance of this project of continually inventing a better, more effective, more usable web under the W3C is also a utopian project. This is intended to be a complimentary assessment. Aiming at Utopia is good. The author shares the opinion of likely many involved with W3C that we aim at technical competence and aim to achieve a Utopian web. It is why, while most of the Web Platform Design Principles are technical (in some cases quite detailed technical) guidance, but the Principles include section 1 and 1.1. While almost everything else can be construed as mostly technical constraints (though one can argue privacy considerations sit on a line between ethical and technical), the principle to put the user first is an ethical point that grounds the document. It is important; it is vital. It is perhaps as influential to how we should approach building the web as the entire [Ethical Web Principles](https://www.w3.org/TR/ethical-web-principles/#privacy). 

The Principles [intend](https://github.com/w3ctag/design-principles/issues/535) to include entities that build out large, multi-author, sites with substantial scale as authors in this priority. On its face, this seems reasonable; publishers are authors on the web like any other. 

In reality, the constraints of the broader non-internet world invade our considerations. Publishers are the invisible substrate of considerations across the W3C, even when no representative of the organization participates. The reason is that, while the web gestures at a future of no gatekeepers, free information, and free unlimited connection, it must be grounded in a reality of modern, capitalist society. 

The author does not intend to talk deeply about the nature of economics and the many theories within. One can view the current market-based economy as the end of history, an eternal present we will operate in. One can view the present as a bridge to the future of a luxury, moneyless, paradise. Readers may imagine any number of things in between. In either case, the present must be dealt with as it is: with an eye towards how we create a web that creates a better future–whatever it might look like. In the present, most of the money that flows to authors does so through some concept of publishers. In the present, the world of publishers creates particular considerations that the W3C broadly and continually considers across many vectors, and appropriately so. Let’s consider some of these.

Publisher–as a type of entity–is visible in the form of video hosting sites of all types. The efficiencies of the video and audio codecs and evolution of how they work in the web satisfies needs of end users, but it also is on behalf of publishers, because large entities must take on the scope and cost of hosting some of the most expensive types of files to store and stream. This work is rightfully a priority. 

Fraudulent users are a concern for services that users use, but not for users themselves. Antifraud measures actually act against a number of pro-user tools, the most obvious being unsubscribe automation tools (a broadening category of products). Antifraud technology also helps publishers keep their sites serving users and not bots, for cost efficiency and ad revenue reasons. However, the W3C does prioritize antifraud work, even if it does not directly benefit users. 

Publishers are one of the reasons we consider improvements to login via Federated ID. In a world of free information and free access there is much less of a need for login. The rise of paywalls has gated off information and made the issue of a retainable consistent log-in experience extremely vital to the modern web, where sub-par existing authentication flows have made it less accessible than perhaps any time in its history since we left behind dial-up modems. This should be solved.

The very invention of the technologies that would become the internet are deeply concerned with access and flow of information. Information about the world around them is one of the main reasons why users access the web. These users acquire that information from authors. There is a desirable growth of independent authors, but under current conditions it is inevitable that authors form coalitions to share the burdens of: hosting, user management, and a variety of professional services that make their lives easier, such as liability insurance, general legal services, and (in some countries) health insurance. Co-ops are already forming for this very reason. Individual authors and groups of authors who become publishers, along with large corporate publishers, are increasingly turning to registration–and pay-walls–in order to continue to maintain the material conditions that allow them to provide this information to users. 

In 2019 the average cost of a subscription to a news media site was $4.06, adjusted for inflation ([https://www.admonsters.com/everything-you-always-wanted-to-know-about-paywalls/](https://www.admonsters.com/everything-you-always-wanted-to-know-about-paywalls/)). Today, in 2026, the average cost of a subscription to a news media site is $23.60 ([https://www.websiteplanet.com/blog/2025-paywall-index-a-data-driven-study-across-industries/](https://www.websiteplanet.com/blog/2025-paywall-index-a-data-driven-study-across-industries/)). During that same period global debt has increased substantially ([https://www.imf.org/external/datamapper/GDD/2025%20Global%20Debt%20Monitor.pdf](https://www.imf.org/external/datamapper/GDD/2025%20Global%20Debt%20Monitor.pdf)). 

Since 2019, wealth inequality globally has increased substantially ([https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution\_of\_wealth\#Gini\_Coefficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_of_wealth#Gini_Coefficient)), but so has the price of access to important information that is regularly used by the users of the web. At the same time, users generally express a willingness to pay for access at rates below the average pricing offered by publications (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paywall\#Reception). 

If ad blocking is the [world’s biggest boycott](https://doc.searls.com/2023/11/11/how-is-the-worlds-biggest-boycott-doing/) (somewhere between 20 and 50% of users, depending on who you ask), then perhaps rejection of paywalls constitutes the world’s other biggest boycott. Up to 60% of users ([https://digiday.com/media/publishers-still-find-it-challenging-to-measure-readers-bypassing-their-paywalls/](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-still-find-it-challenging-to-measure-readers-bypassing-their-paywalls/), [https://allaboutcookies.org/are-paywalls-effective](https://allaboutcookies.org/are-paywalls-effective), [https://www.amediaoperator.com/analysis/over-half-of-readers-attempt-to-circumvent-publishers-paywalls/](https://www.amediaoperator.com/analysis/over-half-of-readers-attempt-to-circumvent-publishers-paywalls/) ) reject paywalls by either leaving sites that present them or using various means to bypass them. Considering these statistics, it is possible that users find logging in to sites even more objectionable than advertisements on them.  

Only 17% of global users have a willingness to pay for news ([https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2024/how-much-do-people-pay-online-news-what-might-encourage-others-pay](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2024/how-much-do-people-pay-online-news-what-might-encourage-others-pay)). Almost 70% of respondents to one survey said  they share access to streaming services ([https://www.securedatarecovery.com/blog/password-sharing-by-state](https://www.securedatarecovery.com/blog/password-sharing-by-state)). 

A 2020 study by [Brave](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.01406) found: 

1\. “Possible risks include reducing societal access to news and information and the privacy harms of the increased user tracking needed to enforce paywalls.”  
2\. Paywalls were becoming increasingly privacy invasive, with more deploying fingerprinting technology.  
3\. Paywalls do not substantially decrease invasive tracking.   
4\. Paywall use is increasing over time.

The study’s assessment of other literature concluded that the current state of digital advertising cannot alone meet the revenue needs of news publishers, which is also dropping. These trends all seem to have continued since 2020\. 

Additionally, it seems very likely that decreased access to news impacts user behavior in meaningful ways ( [https://ang.scholars.harvard.edu/sites/g/files/omnuum4186/files/2026-07/paywalls\_07102026.pdf](https://ang.scholars.harvard.edu/sites/g/files/omnuum4186/files/2026-07/paywalls_07102026.pdf) ), while there are some indications that the change in the types of users accessing news sites may impact news coverage towards less accountability reporting ( [https://academic.oup.com/pnasnexus/article/4/1/pgae511/7960035?login=false](https://academic.oup.com/pnasnexus/article/4/1/pgae511/7960035?login=false) ).    

It is notable that during the last few years–and projected into the future–the expenditure of digital advertising is increasing. ​​It is projected to reach $710 billion dollars by the end of this year ( [https://finance.yahoo.com/news/digital-ad-spending-market-size-123300420.html?guccounter=1\&guce\_referrer=aHR0cHM6Ly9rYWdpLmNvbS8\&guce\_referrer\_sig=AQAAAH1AOjU1XdV4kQERR2ljyygp64lO5\_nKDGHrT4CdbwEGUXZH5NK43BAfZ9eO6-f19MGdrAljHdgZsqr6MDQE-DXksTMk02LagBf2TVmsDuuw6FcPYLJkG2o5-OmIT4-KuEbfBkV7OFYKYXsFFfBgoJzgbJM4Gwk3k3bpqTyuG5yJ](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/digital-ad-spending-market-size-123300420.html?guccounter=1&guce_referrer=aHR0cHM6Ly9rYWdpLmNvbS8&guce_referrer_sig=AQAAAH1AOjU1XdV4kQERR2ljyygp64lO5_nKDGHrT4CdbwEGUXZH5NK43BAfZ9eO6-f19MGdrAljHdgZsqr6MDQE-DXksTMk02LagBf2TVmsDuuw6FcPYLJkG2o5-OmIT4-KuEbfBkV7OFYKYXsFFfBgoJzgbJM4Gwk3k3bpqTyuG5yJ) ). At the same time, buyers are increasingly concerned with fraud and adjacency with bad web content ([https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/report-highlights-key-concerns-among-digital-ad-buyers/733374/](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/report-highlights-key-concerns-among-digital-ad-buyers/733374/) ) .

Why is digital display spending up, while news publisher digital display revenue is down? Why should a user care? 

News media publishers have a high exposure and, therefore, responsibility to users. Other parts of the web have an increasingly unethical set of tools to deploy ads because they can get away with abusing users as low-touch or sometimes even fraudulent enterprises. 

Publishers stand at a unique market disadvantage within the context of the internet. They have a greater responsibility and accountability to users than most entities running advertisements, not just because of the close relationship, but also [because a bad ad experience depreciates the value of their product](https://www.geoedge.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/43204_AdM-GeoEdge-Playbook-June-2023_v2-1.pdf?sbrc=1mrU6KBtYlsEmpBFcECEYOQ%3D%3D$qsOrJpBd1BwTqZMtx2npUg%3D%3D): information. This requires extremely [difficult to maintain](https://digiday.com/media/top-publishers-still-stuck-distributing-fake-news/) higher standards over ad display. Because they are suppliers they have very little control over how ad technology works on their site and–for the majority of user encounters–it works badly, invasively, or both. 

All of this leads us to the current situation: users want news information, users need news information because without it society gets worse, but that information is increasingly locked away behind payment-forcing gating that users have less-and-less capability to access. The reason that information is more difficult to access is because the ad side of the business is less successful, despite increasing money flowing into digital ads. This is because it is harder-and-harder to run ads safely on the web; safely for the user and publisher. It is even more difficult for the buyer. Increasing the access to information by making it easier to book advertisements safely, clearly, and privately is very much in the interest of the user, the ad tech landscape, publishers, buyers and society. 

A decade of arms race between users, publishers, ad tech, fraud, and outright criminals has not resolved the fundamental issues. The war between parties has torn apart the web. Legitimate content is difficult to access and distribute. Users face increasing risks from bad ad tech that often runs through bad sites they use to get data locked behind paywalls. These sites have [all the power and none of the responsibility](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/02/wikipedia-bans-archive-today-after-site-executed-ddos-and-altered-web-captures/) to deform the infosphere. The news media industry is generally agreed to be doing worse than ever. 

The W3C has a responsibility to users to build an internet for them. Ad tech has managed to burn a path of both innovation and anti-user behavior through the web since its inception. This is why the Private Ad Technology group exists: we recognize the status quo cannot continue. Users are being pushed away from the current web. If this continues, it may become a minority player in our global society, ceding user attention to closed unaccountable and unobservable spaces. So we must create brightly-lit paths for advertising to function safely and privately online or face a closed up, irrelevant, anti-user web. This is why PAT has incubated and developed Attribution, the first in what we hope are a series of private, user-controllable, clear-to-use paths for advertising on the open web. The goal is a web that can continue to serve the most users, for as often and as long as possible.
